# Field Collection Protocol

## Photo Capture

Collect each inspection as a small group, not one isolated image:

- Hive exterior and entrance.
- One close entrance image.
- One full frame/top-bar image.
- One closeup of the suspected sign: webbing, larvae, slimeout, mite, deformed wing, fecal staining, ants, or bearding cluster.
- Optional normal comparison frame from the same hive or apiary.

Use ordinary farmer phones when possible. The model must learn real lighting, blur, dust, shadows, and camera quality.

## Required Context

Record these fields at collection time:

- County and nearest town/area.
- Hive type: Langstroth, KTBH, traditional log, or unknown.
- Season context: drought/dearth, nectar flow, rainy period, post-harvest, unknown.
- Time of day and approximate temperature if relevant.
- Whether the frame/top bar was removed.
- What the beekeeper observed: stores dropping, quiet hive, bearding, ants, slime, webbing, dead bees, absconding, or normal.
- Any recent intervention: harvest, feeding, pesticide use, hive move, fire/smoke, disturbance.

## Audio Capture

For audio, collect 30-60 second clips:

- Place phone/mic at a consistent distance from entrance or inside inspection cover when safe.
- Record wind/rain/human speech notes separately; do not hide noisy conditions.
- Pair audio with at least one photo and an inspection label when possible.

## Safety and Ethics

- Get consent from the beekeeper for data use.
- Avoid faces, phone numbers, precise GPS coordinates, and private household details unless there is explicit consent.
- Never stage unsafe pesticide use for data collection.
- Label uncertain cases as uncertain; do not force a disease diagnosis without evidence.
